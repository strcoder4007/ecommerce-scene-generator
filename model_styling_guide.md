E‑commerce Fashion Image Style Guide 

1) Gist / Intent

Purpose: Product-first fashion catalogue photography that still feels aspirational — lifestyle not pure studio. The clothing is the hero, the environment sells mood (vacation, relaxed luxury).

Key vibes: warm, sunny, polished, approachable, slightly editorial. Not gritty street-style; not dramatic high-fashion. Think "vacation wardrobe lookbook".



2) Poses & Model Direction (what to ask the model to do)

Natural weight-shifts: one hip popped, weight on one leg (S-curve). Legs uncrossed for product fit clarity.

Torso twist & shoulder tilt to show silhouette + neckline.

Hands: soft, relaxed — fingers lightly touching fabric/hip/railing; avoid clenched fists.

Head: slight tilt or chin-down to engage the camera; neutral/soft smile or confident relaxed expression.

Motion shots: gentle step forward, light skirt sway, hair toss — small motion to show drape.

Shots to prioritize: full-body vertical

3) Camera, Lens & Framing

Sensor: full-frame or APS-C with equivalent fields.

Focal length guidance:

Full-body / environmental portraits: 35mm–50mm.

Aperture: f/2.8–f/5.6 depending on desired background separation. Often f/3.2–f/4 gives subject pop while keeping enough context.

Shutter / ISO: daylight ISO 100–200; shutter 1/125–1/500 (faster if handheld/action).

White balance: daylight (5200–6000K) for midday; warmer (around 4800–5200K) for golden hour.

Orientation: mostly vertical (9:16 / 3:4) for e‑commerce and social; some horizontal for banners.

4) Lighting & Time of Day

Primary: natural daylight. Preferred windows:

Mid-morning / late-afternoon (golden hour mood) for warm, soft light.

Bright midday but use fill (reflector or soft fill flash) to avoid harsh shadows on face.

Fill: 1–2 stop fill from reflector or TTL flash to even skin highlights and preserve detail in eyes.

Direction: slightly front-side (10–45°) to model to sculpt the face and fabric.

5) Color, Contrast & Post-processing

Color palette: warm skin tones, saturated garment colors (rich jewel tones and vibrant prints), vivid blues/teals in water/sky.

Contrast: medium — boost micro-contrast in fabric, keep skin smooth.

Saturation & vibrance: increase overall vibrance, selectively boost blues/teals and reds while preserving natural skin tones.

Tone: slightly warm midtones, gentle highlight roll-off, mild filmic shadows (not crushed).

Retouching: clean but natural — even skin, remove stray hairs, maintain skin texture. Sharpen edges of fabrics and jewelry; reduce shine on forehead/nose with subtle dodge & burn.

6) Styling & Wardrobe Notes

Clothing presented wrinkle-free, well-steamed. Emphasize drape and fit (e.g., waistline, hemline, neckline).

Accessories minimal but intentional: hoop earrings, bracelets, simple sandals or heels. Props like straw bags or railings used sparingly.

Hair & makeup: natural glam — matte/soft skin, defined brows, neutral lip. Hair either loose waves or tidy pony/bun depending on look.

7) Composition patterns used repeatedly

Rule of thirds: model centered or slightly off-center, horizon often low for beach shots.

Leading lines: railings, boardwalks, pavements to draw the eye to the model.

Negative space: leave breathing room above head and in direction model faces to allow cropping for banners.

8) Technical deliverables / output specs

Primary output: vertical 9:16 (1440x2560) high-res versions.

Color profile: sRGB for web; keep master TIFFs in ProPhoto or Adobe RGB for archives.

Sharpen for web at final size; save unflattened masters and retouched TIFFs.



9) Post-production checklist (for editor)

Global color balance: set WB, adjust tones.

Exposure: ensure shadows and highlights recoverable.

Skin: frequency separation if needed, avoid plastic look.

Fabric: enhance texture and color; dodge & burn to emphasize seams and folds.

Eyes/teeth: gentle brightening only.

Output resize & sharpen per target sizes.
